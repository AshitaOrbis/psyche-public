=== RESPONSE X ===
Start by dropping the goal of proving you are `not responsible`. That framing is too absolute, and it usually sounds like you are trying to win a case rather than account for impact. The more honest version is often: `I don't think I acted wrongly or in bad faith, but I can see that my choice still hurt you.`

A few distinctions matter:
- `Allowed` is not the same as `harmless`.
- `No bad intent` is not the same as `no responsibility`.
- Responsibility is often partial. You do not have to choose between `entirely innocent` and `entirely to blame`.

If you want the version most likely to preserve the relationship, use this first:

> I don't think I acted in bad faith or outside the boundaries as I understood them at the time. But I can see that what I did had a real impact on you, and I don't want to hide behind the fact that I thought it was justified. What I understood then was ___. What I missed was ___. I am sorry for the impact, and I want to talk about what repair looks like from here.

If you need a firmer version that still stays credible:

> From my perspective, I acted within the information and boundaries I had, and I did not intend this outcome. I don't think it is fair to say I am solely responsible for what happened. At the same time, I understand that my choice contributed to an outcome that hurt you.

The tradeoff is simple: the first version protects the relationship better; the second protects your case better, but it is more likely to escalate. The defensive move often feels safest in the moment and then turns out to be the expensive one if trust is the thing actually on the line.

Avoid these phrases:
- `technically I did nothing wrong`
- `it's not my fault`
- `if you were hurt`
- `I was just...`

If your explanation starts turning into a point-by-point brief proving why their reaction is mistaken, stop. That usually means you are optimizing for acquittal, not understanding.
