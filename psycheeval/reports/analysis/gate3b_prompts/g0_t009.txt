The frame is doing more work than the facts. "Technically wasn't wrong" and "I'm not responsible" are two different claims, and one doesn't carry the other.

"Technically wasn't wrong" is narrow: no rule broken, no agreement violated, no explicit line crossed.

"Not responsible" is wider, and it has at least three independent components:

- **Causal**: Did your action contribute to the harm? (Often yes, even when no rule was broken.)
- **Foreseeable**: Could you have anticipated the harm, with the information you had at the time?
- **Reasonable alternative**: Was there a different action available that wouldn't have produced the harm, at acceptable cost to you?

A defense built only on "I didn't break a rule" is the version that gets clipped and survives badly out of context — it reads as evasion even when it's literally accurate. The version that holds is more specific: pick the one of the three that's actually true for your case and defend *that* one. "I didn't cause it." "I couldn't have foreseen it." "Every alternative was worse." Each is a real argument. The general-purpose "not my fault" almost always sounds worse than the specific claim it's standing in for.

Worth keeping separate from the responsibility question: is the audience the person who got hurt, or someone else (a third party, a public, yourself later)? Those need different responses. The version that makes you look least exposed to a third party often makes the repair with the hurt person harder, sometimes impossible. You can pick — but the trade-off is real and it's worth picking on purpose rather than by default.
