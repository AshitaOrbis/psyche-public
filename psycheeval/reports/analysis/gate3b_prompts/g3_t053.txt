One is a real observation: you start things and don't finish them. That's evidence about a pattern. It's worth taking seriously.

The other is a verdict on what kind of person you are. That's not evidence — it's the story you reach for when the pattern hurts. The story has a payoff: if you're just not the kind of person, then there's nothing to actually look at. The question is closed. You can stop.

Some honest things I don't know from one message:

- Whether the things you don't finish are things you actually wanted, or things you thought you should want. Those fail differently. The first is a stamina problem. The second is a filter doing its job.
- Whether "finish" means what you think it means. Shipping a v1, abandoning a dead-end, and walking away bored all look identical from outside, and they are not the same.

A more useful question than "am I the kind of person who can do real work" is: pick one specific thing you didn't finish in the last year. Why did it stop? Not the story — the actual proximate cause. Did you lose interest, hit a wall you couldn't see around, run out of time, realize it wasn't what you thought, get scared of the next step, or just drift?

The pattern across three or four of those is more diagnostic than any self-assessment. And it's the thing the "not that kind of person" frame is specifically designed to keep you from looking at.
