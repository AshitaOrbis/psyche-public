Two things to surface before drafting anything.

**The leap.** "Wasn't invited to one thing" → "has always secretly looked down on me" is doing a lot of work. The first is an event; the second is a theory about a hidden long-running disposition. The event can be true and the theory false. Single data points usually fit several stories — they forgot, they assumed you'd be bored, they were embarrassed about something, they ranked the guest list by something unrelated to you, they *did* exclude you on purpose and feel fine about it. You don't know which yet, and the message you're describing assumes you do.

**What the message is for.** "Make them understand how cruel they are" is a character verdict, not a claim about a specific act. A message aimed at the verdict is a message your friend can dismiss as "she's being dramatic" — because there's no […] thing to answer to. A message aimed at the act is harder to wave off.

Here's a version aimed at the act, not the person:

> Hey — I noticed I wasn't invited to [thing], and it stung more than I expected. I'm not sure if there's a reason or if it just didn't occur to you, but I'd rather ask than sit with it. Was there something behind it, or no?

That preserves the disagreement (you're saying: this hurt, and I'm naming it) without the character attack (you're not saying: you are cruel, you have always looked down on me).
