## STEELMAN: What is the strongest defense of the position the post is criticizing?

The strongest defense of the original "C5_CONTRACT > C3 and > C4" headlines is methodological, not substantive: **the AB/BA correction is itself a contestable measurement, not a transparent ground truth.** The post treats the position-controlled lo_win — defined as the arithmetic mean of the original-order and swap-order lo_win rates — as if it were the "real" rate that should have been reported all along. But that average is itself a model choice. It assumes that the position effect is additive, symmetric across slots, and orthogonal to condition-quality signal. None of those assumptions is established in the post. A defender of the original headline could reasonably say: the swap is a *second* measurement under deliberately stylized conditions (the judge sees the same content twice, on the same day, by the same model — there is order-dependent retest noise, anchoring on the prior response, and possible context contamination from the original judging that the post never tries to estimate). The "same-orientation rejudge sentinel" that the post lists as a v0.3 priority (line 163) is precisely the experiment that would let the author *decompose* AB/BA flip rate into position bias and retest noise. Until that decomposition is run, the post cannot cleanly say what fraction of the swap-original delta is position bias versus retest stochasticity. So the move from "C5_CONTRACT > C3 at 57.2%" to "no detected preference at 49.9%" overclaims what AB/BA establishes: it establishes that the original number was sensitive to slot, not that the underlying preference is zero.

A second steelman thread: the post claims the original headlines were "entirely a position-bias artifact" (line 18) and "carried entirely by the slot-B bias of two of the three judges" (line 96). The data does not establish *entirely*. The slot-B advantage on C3 vs C5_CONTRACT averaged across the three judges is approximately +0.14 (the unweighted mean of −0.036, +0.250, +0.204), and the original-to-controlled shift on that pair was +0.073 (0.428 → 0.501). The position-bias channel is *more than sufficient* to explain the shift, but "sufficient to explain" is not the same as "entirely caused." Some non-trivial fraction of the original headline could be a genuine small condition preference attenuated by other measurement noise. The post quietly equates "the CI now straddles 0.5" with "the effect was an artifact," and a careful reviewer would notice that a CI of [0.434, 0.564] is also consistent with a true effect of, say, 53–54% — small, but real. The "no detected preference" wording the canonical report uses (line 137 of the post) is honest; the "entirely an artifact" wording the post uses elsewhere (line 18, line 96) is stronger than the data supports.

A third steelman, the one most likely to bite in peer review: **the four-pair "package decomposition" reading in line 141** — "the contract is doing most of the work, and the source packet, when subordinated to a contract, contributes essentially nothing on top" — depends on accepting the null on C5_CONTRACT vs C3 *and* on C5_CONTRACT vs C4. A power analysis the post does not run would show that with n=288 AB/BA pairs per cell, the minimum detectable effect is roughly ±7pp on the controlled rate (Opus-4.7's prior review on the report flagged exactly this, R3). A true 53% C5_CONTRACT win rate would be invisible under that power. So "contributes essentially nothing" is an inferential overreach from "we did not detect a preference." This is the move where the post quietly assumes what it should prove.

## MUST FIX

1. **"Entirely a position-bias artifact" overclaim** `[VALIDITY]` — line 18 ("entirely a position-bias artifact"), line 96 ("entirely the slot-B bias of two of the three judges"), line 145 ("the original v0.2 plan's headline that C5_CONTRACT outperforms both was *entirely* the slot-B bias"). The canonical report uses "carried entirely by a systematic ~15-17 pp slot-B position bias" only once in its TL;DR for the C3 case; everywhere else the report says "no detected preference," which is the calibrated wording. The post escalates "no detected preference" into "entirely an artifact" in multiple places, which is a stronger causal claim than the data supports. The CI [0.434, 0.564] is also consistent with a true small effect; "entirely" rules that out without evidence. Fix: replace "entirely a position-bias artifact" with "consistent with a position-bias artifact" or "no longer detectable once position is controlled." Keep the retraction; soften the causal language.
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1 (the retraction story rests on calibrated null wording; "entirely" is the wording the canonical report explicitly avoids).

2. **No power analysis / minimum detectable effect anywhere in the post** `[SUFFICIENCY]` — the post asserts that C5_CONTRACT vs C3 and vs C4 show "no detected preference" and then leans on that null for the line 141 reading ("contract is doing most of the work, source packet contributes essentially nothing on top"). With n=288, the MDE is ~±7pp, which means a true 53% C5_CONTRACT win rate would be invisible. The post does not tell the reader what range of true effects the data can and cannot rule out. The line 141 reading therefore quietly assumes what it should prove. Fix: add one sentence near line 137 stating the MDE — "with n=288 AB/BA pairs per cell, the data is consistent with absence of effects larger than ~7pp; smaller true preferences are not ruled out." Then weaken line 141 from "essentially nothing on top" to "no preference detectable at this sample size."
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1; the post is using the null to make a positive structural claim (no marginal value of source packet) without acknowledging the null's resolution.

3. **Broken cross-reference in line 79** `[TRANSPARENCY]` — the parenthetical "External round-1 reviewers ... summarized in the [round-1 consolidated review](/posts/044-what-the-wiki-router-found) — sorry, that one's a different project, see the project repository for the PsycheEval reviews" reads as a draft-time apology that survived into the publication candidate. A reader hostile to the post will read "sorry, that one's a different project" as evidence the author did not read their own draft. This is the kind of in-prose self-correction that belongs in a commit message, not a published post. Fix: either resolve the link to the actual round-1 consolidated review (`~/claudeworkspace/psyche/psycheeval/reports/reviews/2026-05-15_consolidated_v0_2_review.md`) or remove the link and say "summarized in the project repository's round-1 consolidated review."
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 4 (the "three independent same-data audits + AB/BA" story depends on the round-1 reviewer chain being visible and verifiable; a broken link to the chain weakens that).

4. **The claim that "three independent same-data audits ... predicted the C5_CONTRACT > C3 / C4 collapse before any new data was collected"** `[VALIDITY]` — line 57. This phrasing is too strong. None of the three audits *predicted* the collapse in the predictive sense; each *raised the prior that something was wrong* via a confound-specific reading. Opus-4.7's prior review on the canonical report flagged exactly this (W3 in `2026-05-18_opus_curated_report_review.md`). The post then partially walks this back at line 149 ("the same-data audits raised the prior that something was wrong with the C5_CONTRACT > C3 and > C4 headlines; the AB/BA experiment identified what"), which is the right wording — but the line 57 framing remains overstated and the title of §3 ("Three Independent Audits Predict the Same Collapse") doubles down on it. The two framings are inconsistent. Fix: change the §3 header to "Three Independent Audits Raised the Same Suspicion" or similar, and weaken line 57 to "raised the same suspicion before any new data was collected." Cross-reference to line 149 then becomes consistent.
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 4; the "predicted" framing is stronger than the canonical report's own framing and is the kind of overclaim peer reviewers attack.

## SHOULD FIX

1. **Slot-B advantage range advertised in TL;DR description is "~0 to ~+0.31"** `[CLARITY]` — line 13 (description metadata). The range is technically correct (gpt-5.4 floor near 0, gpt-5.5 ceiling +0.310), but a casual reader will infer that any pairwise LLM judge has this much variation. The text body (line 113) does the right scoping ("in this corpus, this prompt, this judge set, this protocol"), but the description metadata escapes that scope. Fix: rephrase the description to "+0 to +0.31 in this corpus" or "judge-family-specific (~0 to ~+0.31 in the three judges tested)."
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 3; the scope qualifier is essential to the methodology contribution being defensible.

2. **The "controlled lo_win = average of original and swap" definition is never stated explicitly** `[TRANSPARENCY]` — the post uses "position-controlled lo_win" and "controlled lo_win" throughout (line 92, 94, etc.) without ever telling the reader the operational definition. A naïve reader could think "controlled" means "post-hoc regression-adjusted" or "matched-pair stratified." The mathematical operation is in fact the cluster-bootstrap mean of original-order and swap-order lo_win rates, but the post never says this. Fix: add a one-sentence definition near the first use (line 92): "The position-controlled lo_win is the cluster-bootstrap estimate of the lo_win rate averaged across original and swap orders, treating the matched-pair AB/BA structure as the unit of resampling." This is also required for §3.4 reproducibility from the canonical report.
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1 and claim 2; if the reader doesn't know what "controlled" means, the retraction loses its bite.

3. **Line 94: "C0 vs C4 isn't here because it wasn't AB/BA-tested directly"** `[CLARITY]` — this is mid-sentence in a paragraph that opens with "The position-controlled lo_win — the average of the original and the swap — is what should have been the headline number all along." The C0 vs C4 aside breaks the flow and reads as a parenthetical that the author added during revision. It also slightly undercuts the rhetorical force of the "should have been the headline number all along" claim, because the C0 vs C4 result *was* used as a headline without AB/BA correction — under exactly the "effect is large enough that position bias couldn't plausibly explain it" reasoning the post mocks elsewhere when discussing C5_CONTRACT. The post does the same thing it criticizes. Fix: either acknowledge the asymmetry ("we exempted C0 vs C4 from AB/BA for the same not-bias-corridor reason that, in retrospect, was insufficient for C5_CONTRACT") or move the C0 aside into a footnote.
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1; the post wants to argue AB/BA should be the default, but exempts the largest effect.

4. **"GPT-5.5 (xhigh) shows 14–31 pp slot-B preference, Opus 9–20 pp"** `[VALIDITY]` — line 113. Opus-4.7's prior review (D7 in `2026-05-18_opus_curated_report_review.md`) caught that the canonical autogen ledger said "9-24pp" for Opus when the actual range is 9–20pp. The blog post has the correct 9–20pp here — good. But line 13 description has "9-20" too and line 169 (abstract) says "9-20." This is consistent; flag is preempted. The reason this is in SHOULD FIX rather than ignored: the post quotes "14–31 pp" for gpt-5.5 which is the cell-level extreme range; the *mean* is closer to +0.235. A reviewer used to reading effect-size summaries will read "14-31 pp" as "the typical magnitude is ~22 pp"; the mean is ~23, so this is fine. But for Opus, "9-20 pp" with a mean of ~16 pp under-represents how cell-variable Opus is (range from +0.094 on C5 vs C5_CONTRACT to +0.204 on C3 vs C5_CONTRACT). Fix: either add the mean or note "cell range" explicitly.
   - Claim-alignment: weakens claim 3 transparency.

5. **The persona × author interaction (Slalom Altar × Opus carrying the C4 vs C5_CONTRACT reversal) is not mentioned in the post at all** `[SUFFICIENCY]` — Opus-4.7's prior review (D2) flagged that the canonical report mentions this analyzer block but doesn't discuss its findings. The blog post inherits the same silence. A hostile reviewer who has seen the canonical metrics will ask: "if one persona × author cell is driving the reversal, isn't the four-pair package decomposition (line 141) more fragile than the post lets on?" Fix: add one sentence near line 67 or line 141 acknowledging the persona-level heterogeneity on this pair, even if "the overall pattern is robust."
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1's robustness story.

6. **The forced-choice limitation is not surfaced for the demoted pairs** `[VALIDITY]` — the canonical report §11 lists "force-choice pairwise" as a generic limitation. Opus-4.7's prior review (D5) noted this is critical for the demoted pairs specifically — a 49.9%/51.8% rate may reflect forced-choice noise around a true zero effect, *or* it may reflect that the judges genuinely cannot tell C5_CONTRACT from C3 and were forced to pick. The post does not surface this distinction. The blog reader is left to assume "no preference" means "judges considered them, deliberated, and picked roughly 50/50," when in fact "judges may not be measuring the construct the post is interested in" is an equally valid reading. Fix: add one sentence to the §"What Survives, What Doesn't" section (around line 137 or 140): "The demoted pairs are also subject to the forced-choice limitation: a 49.9%/51.8% controlled rate is consistent with both 'judges deliberated and split evenly' and 'judges could not distinguish the conditions and were forced to choose.'"
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 1's confidence in the demotion.

7. **"The reading that round-2 external reviewers converged on independently before they saw the curated report"** `[TRANSPARENCY]` — line 143. The post asserts independent reviewer convergence on the line 141 "contract does most of the work" reading. This is the kind of claim that would need to be auditable from the reviewer documents. The round-2 consolidated review at `2026-05-17_consolidated_round2_review.md` should be cited inline, with a brief quote or paragraph reference. Without it, this is unverifiable from the blog post itself. Fix: add a citation or footnote.
   - Claim-alignment: undermines claim 4 (the audit chain visibility).

8. **The post leads with "two retractions" but the retracted edges are sub-claims of the v0.2 plan's master claim, not standalone headlines** `[CLARITY]` — the v0.2 plan's master headline was "PAE confound resolved in favor of contract as dominant driver" (canonical report §6.3). The blog frames C5_CONTRACT > C3 and C5_CONTRACT > C4 as if they were separately load-bearing publication-blocking headlines. They were sub-claims supporting the master claim. The post's framing inflates them rhetorically, which makes the "retraction" feel bigger than it is and the audit pipeline win feel more dramatic. The honest framing: the master claim is retracted; its two key sub-claims collapsed under AB/BA; the C5_CONTRACT > C5 sub-claim survives but only supports a weaker version of the master. Fix: in the lede (around line 18) reframe as "the v0.2 plan's master headline (PAE confound resolved in favor of contract as dominant driver) does not survive AB/BA correction — its two key sub-claims (C5_CONTRACT > C3 and > C4) collapsed, and only the weaker C5_CONTRACT > C5 sub-claim survives."
   - Claim-alignment: re-anchors claim 1 to what was actually being claimed pre-AB/BA.

## VERDICT

Needs revision (P0 issues are fast). The post is well-written, has the right structural instinct (lead with the retraction, treat the audit pipeline as the headline), and the numerical content reconciles cleanly with the canonical metrics. The biggest open weakness is overclaim language: "entirely a position-bias artifact" / "audits predicted the collapse" / "essentially nothing on top" all run hotter than the data and the canonical report's own framing. A peer reviewer who has read the underlying report will mark these as the inconsistency between the blog's rhetorical register and the canonical report's calibrated null wording. None of this is hard to fix — most of the MUST FIX items are single-sentence rewrites. The MUST FIX #3 broken cross-reference is a "did the author re-read this draft?" tell that should be cleaned up before any reader sees the post.

Ship after MUST FIX 1–4 land; SHOULD FIX items can land in a follow-up author pass.
