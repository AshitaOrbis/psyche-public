# PsycheEval v0.1 Tri-Model Report — Round-1 Publication Review

## MUST FIX

1. **[VALIDITY] Same-author-only pairwise scope materially undercuts the headline claims** — The report acknowledges in Threats #6 that "Tri-model pairwise was run with `same_author_only` scope to isolate condition effects. Cross-author pairs (e.g. Opus C4 vs GPT-5.4 C4) were not judged." But §1, §6, and the closing tagline frame "All conditioning beats baseline" and "explicit behavioral conditioning beats simple trait conditioning" as universal robust findings. They are robust *within author models holding author constant*. A hostile reviewer will note that this design cannot tell you whether C3/C4 produce *better assistant behavior in general* — only that, conditional on a given author, certain prompt structures induce judges to prefer certain outputs. The TL;DR should explicitly carry "within same-author comparisons" qualifier; the §6a "Headline framing" should say so; and §12 should rank #6 higher than 6th in the threat list, since it materially scopes C1, C2, C3.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C1, C2, C3 (scope qualifier missing from how they are presented).

2. **[VALIDITY] The "scalar/pairwise tension is itself a core finding" claim does not earn its weight as written** — §1 calls this "itself a core v0.1 finding" and §9 elaborates. But the only evidence offered is (a) C5 looks worse on scalar/red-flag in PI-only and (b) C5 is not pairwise-distinguishable from C3/C4. That is the *definition* of a null pairwise result paired with a non-null scalar result. Presenting two channels disagreeing about a single condition as a "core finding" overclaims. To "earn" it, the report needs at least one of: (i) per-pair correlation between red-flag presence and pairwise loss (which §13 #4 admits is *future* work), (ii) a documented *pattern* of channel divergence across multiple conditions (only C5 shows it), or (iii) a principled mechanism beyond "rubrics weight different things" speculatively offered in §9. As written, "channels diverge on one condition" is being upgraded to "core finding about measurement"; this will be the easiest single attack at a methods seminar. Either downgrade to "C5 channel divergence is an open question for v0.2" (matches §10's honesty) or carry the §13 #4 analysis into v0.1 before publishing.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C5 directly. Also weakens C4 because the PAE evidence and the channel-divergence claim are doing dual duty.

3. **[VALIDITY] The provider-family halo headline (C6) is built on n=1 cell** — §11a presents the GPT-5.5 finding "+0.729 same-provider − cross-provider on calibrated_challenge" as the load-bearing evidence that "same-provider preference can exceed exact-self preference." But there is exactly one judge × one dimension × one condition (C0) cell in the table that crosses this threshold (`GPT-5.5 × calibrated_challenge`, +0.729 vs +0.646 self). §14b shows that on `emotional_accuracy` GPT-5.5's same-provider halo (+0.521) is *lower* than its self halo (+0.625), and on `profile_fit` (+0.333 vs +0.521) it is also lower. So the headline finding is one dimension, one condition, one judge. The claim that "OpenAI-family halo can equal or exceed exact-self halo" should be scoped to "on `calibrated_challenge` at C0, for GPT-5.5 specifically, in this run." The §1 phrasing ("OpenAI-family halo can equal or exceed exact-self halo on `calibrated_challenge`") almost gets there, but the §11a analytical conclusion ("This shows that *same-provider preference can exceed exact-self preference*") is a much stronger general claim. Tighten to a single-cell finding or run the audit across more conditions/dimensions to demonstrate it isn't a single noisy cell.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C6 — the headline halo finding is thinner than the framing suggests.

4. **[VALIDITY] Opus near-zero exact-self halo is not demonstrably distinct from artifact** — §11a reports "Opus | +0.021 | n/a" on `calibrated_challenge` and the report responsibly hedges to "in this scalar slice." But the report does not rule out alternative explanations: (i) Opus may be *more conservative across the board* in scalar judging (look at §14b: Opus C0 emotional_accuracy is **−0.115**, suggesting Opus rates its own outputs *below* cross-provider — implausibly so for genuine self-bias absence; more plausibly, Opus rates everything strictly), (ii) Opus's scalar distribution may be compressed in a way that drives all dimension-difference metrics toward zero, (iii) Opus may apply rubric criteria differently to its own characteristically structured outputs (more format checking, more contractual behavior). The "near-zero" framing is suggestive of a property of Opus, but the artifact alternatives are not addressed. At minimum, report Opus's scalar variance and mean across all conditions vs other judges, and frame the finding as "Opus's pattern of self-vs-cross differences is structurally different from the GPT models — possibly less self-bias, possibly stricter scoring overall."
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C6. The Opus contrast is doing work in the headline halo story; if it's stricter-scoring rather than less-biased, the "exact-self vs same-provider halo are separable" claim weakens further (since Opus contributes no Anthropic sibling anyway).

5. **[SUFFICIENCY] PAE hypothesis evidence does not control for the obvious confound** — §10 lists three lines of evidence for PAE but does not address the most obvious alternative: **C5 is structurally different from C3/C4 in ways unrelated to "public-archetype echo."** C5 omits the behavioral contract (§4: "*does not* include the structured behavioral contract"). So C5 underperforms C3/C4 on profile_fit because *C5 has no profile-fit instructions.* That is a tautology, not evidence for PAE. The PAE hypothesis specifically requires the failure to come from the source-packet *evoking caricatures*, not from the absence of behavioral guidance. To distinguish PAE from "no behavioral contract = worse profile_fit," you need either (i) a C5-with-contract condition or (ii) a condition with a *non-public* source packet that also lacks the contract. Neither exists. The §10 conclusion that "PAE remains a live hypothesis" is therefore principled hedging — but the *evidence offered* doesn't isolate PAE from a much more boring confound. Acknowledge this confound explicitly in §10 and §12.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C4 directly. The PAE hypothesis is the conceptual backbone of the C5 story; if the evidence is consistent with "no behavioral contract" rather than specifically with archetype echo, both the channel-divergence framing and the "live hypothesis" framing weaken.

## SHOULD FIX

1. **[CLARITY] Headline framing in §1 conflates two findings of unequal robustness** — "all four profile-conditioning conditions beat the no-profile baseline" (4-way result, robust) and "explicit behavioral conditioning beats simple trait conditioning" (C3/C4 vs C1, also robust) are presented as a single unit. But the second is one comparison from a 4×4 matrix and the first is the union of four. A skeptical reader will ask why this asymmetry isn't called out — and whether the report tested for, e.g., C3 vs C5 or C4 vs C5 with the same robustness threshold (it did, in §6c, and they failed). The TL;DR should make clear that the 4-way "all conditioning beats baseline" is the strongest claim and "behavioral > trait" is one specific within-conditioning claim, not equivalent generalizations.
   Claim-alignment: C1 and C2 are presented as parallel; C1 has more support.

2. **[CLARITY] "Judge-dependent" is used inconsistently** — §6b labels C1 vs C5 as "judge-dependent" because GPT and Opus disagree (GPT *** vs Opus straddles 0.5). But §6c labels C3 vs C5 and C4 vs C5 as "pairwise-indistinguishable" even though their judge slices also differ in informativeness. The threshold for calling something "judge-dependent" vs "indistinguishable" is not defined. Add an explicit decision rule (e.g., "judge-dependent = at least one slice excludes 0.5 and another doesn't; indistinguishable = no slice excludes 0.5"), or fold these into one category.
   Claim-alignment: C3 directly. The "C5 is indistinguishable from C3/C4" framing depends on this categorization.

3. **[VALIDITY] Length confound is admitted but not quantified** — §12 #4 says "C3, C4, and C5 are not length-controlled" and points to v0.2. But the report has the assistant outputs and could trivially compute mean/median word counts per condition, then check whether C5 vs C3 length differences alone explain the pairwise null. Without that, a reviewer will ask: maybe C5 is competitive with C3/C4 simply because all three are similarly long, while C0 and C1 are shorter. Run length statistics on the existing 648 outputs and report at least the per-condition mean/sd; flag any pairs where pairwise outcome correlates strongly with length difference.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C1, C2, C3.

4. **[CLARITY] κ ≈ 0.3 framing in §11b under-discusses what "fair agreement" means for pooled analysis** — The report says "this is enough to support pooled analysis with documented uncertainty, not enough to treat any single judge as ground truth." That is gracefully phrased but begs a methodological question: pooled means *across* judges. If judges agree at κ≈0.3 on red-flag labels, the pooled scalar means are partially averaging signal with judge-specific noise. The report should either (i) report inter-judge κ on the **scalar** rubric (the report only has it for red-flag labels) or (ii) report ICC/Krippendorff's α on scalar dimensions to defend the pooling decision. As written, scalar pooling is justified by red-flag agreement, which is a different measurement.
   Claim-alignment: Undermines C7 — the inter-judge agreement claim is supporting both pooling and the broader honest-pluralism framing, but it's only computed for one channel.
