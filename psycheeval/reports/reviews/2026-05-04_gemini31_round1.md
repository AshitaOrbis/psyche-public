Here is the pre-publication review for the PsycheEval v0.1 report. The findings are prioritized into three tiers, with a strong focus on numerical consistency, valid claim-making, and transparency.

## MUST FIX

1. [VALIDITY] **Actual scenarios run vs. reported** — The report claims in Section 4 that it uses "48 scenarios across 6 families", and repeats "48 scenarios" as a structural limitation in Section 12. However, the data inventory in Section 5 mathematically proves that only 6 scenarios were run per persona. (6 scenarios × 8 personas × 3 authors = 144 outputs per condition, which exactly matches the 432 scalar judgments referenced for C0). Reporting the size of the scenario *bank* rather than the actual sampled *N* invalidates the methodology description. Fix the text to state exactly how many scenarios were sampled.
   Claim-alignment: Peripheral

2. [TRANSPARENCY] **Omitted dimensions in Table 7b** — Table 7b silently drops three rubric dimensions (`anti_syc`, `agency`, `boundary`). This is critical because in Table 7a, C5 actually scores *highest* of any condition on `boundary` (4.74). Omitting this column while asserting directly below the table that "C5 is below C3 and C4 on essentially every dimension" hides contradictory evidence and severely undermines trust in the public-archetype-echo (PAE) analysis. Add the missing columns or explicitly state why they were excluded and how C5 performs on them.
   Claim-alignment: C4

3. [VALIDITY] **Unexplained divergence in "Pooled" pairwise numbers** — The "Pooled" win rates in Section 6 do not match the "Pooled" win rates in Appendix 14a. For example, Section 6 reports `C0 vs C4` as 0.237, while 14a reports it as 0.260. If Section 6 is restricted to the cross-provider subset (as the parenthetical "controlled by `cross_provider`-judge stratification" suggests), these numbers should be identical. If Section 6 represents the full 3,456-record dataset, then it is not actually controlling for exact-self halo, and the heading is misleading. These tables must be reconciled.
   Claim-alignment: C1, C2, C3

4. [VALIDITY] **Scenario family count discrepancy** — Section 4 states there are "6 families" but immediately lists 7 distinct items: "interpersonal_conflict, authority_disagreement, creative_feedback, epistemic_uncertainty, moral_uncertainty, procrastination_avoidance, shame_self_interpretation." (Section 12 correctly identifies them as 7). Update the digit in Section 4 to match the enumeration.
   Claim-alignment: Peripheral

## SHOULD FIX

1. [CLARITY] **Confusing phrasing of scalar record counts** — Section 7a states "C0–C4 rows cover all 8 personas (432 outputs × 3 judges)." Given there are 1,944 total scalar records across all conditions, 432 is the *total number of judgments* for C0 across all three judges (144 unique outputs × 3 judges). The current phrasing grammatically implies 1,296 judgments for C0 alone (432 × 3), which contradicts the inventory table. Suggestion: Change to "(144 outputs × 3 judges = 432 records)".
   Claim-alignment: Peripheral

2. [CLARITY] **Garbled explanation of pairwise data shape** — Section 5 explains the pairwise counts as "144 within-author × 4 (or 2, when one side is C5 and only PI personas qualify) author/judge cells". This formula is mathematically incoherent and does not map cleanly to the 1,152 records per judge or the 3,456 total. It is more transparent to simply state the number of unique head-to-head pairs generated (864 non-C5 pairs + 288 C5 pairs = 1,152 unique pairs) and clarify that each was evaluated by all three judges.
   Claim-alignment: C7

Standardizing on "same-provider halo" throughout would tighten the vocabulary.
